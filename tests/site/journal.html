Three nights in the hills

By Ada Example

We set out on a Thursday with three days of food, a stove that had seen better years and a map that had been folded so often the creases had become paths of their own. The plan was simple: walk the long ridge from the reservoir to the old quarry, sleep where the ground was flat, and come down before the weather turned on Sunday afternoon.

The first night

The first camp was a shelf of short grass above a stream, sheltered on the west by a band of rock. We arrived with an hour of light to spare, which on a short autumn day is a luxury, and spent it doing very little: filling bottles, watching the cloud sit on the far summits, and arguing in a friendly way about whether the stream would be loud enough to keep us awake.

Draft note the stylesheet hides: check the stove's fuel figures before this goes out.

Gear note: the Trail Lamp ran for nineteen hours on one charge over the three nights.

The second night

The second day was the long one, eighteen kilometres over ground that never quite let us find a rhythm. By the time we reached the saddle below the quarry the wind had picked up and the light was going, so we pitched in the lee of a wall and cooked by headtorch. Nobody complained about the pasta, which tells you how hungry we were.

Pull quote: the ridge will still be there.

The third night

Hidden aside about the weather forecast that nobody believed.

On the last night the cloud came down and stayed down. We woke to a world that ended twenty metres from the tent in every direction, packed up slowly, and walked out by compass to the road, where the bus arrived eleven minutes late and felt like the most luxurious thing we had ever seen.
