A night on the ridge

We left the car at the end of the forest road a little after four, when the light was already turning the colour of weak tea. The path climbs steadily through spruce for the first hour, then opens onto a long shoulder of grass where the wind never quite stops. Nobody else was on the hill, which is the whole reason to go in late October.

The tent went up in eleven minutes, which is not a record but is respectable with cold fingers. We pitched it side-on to the wind, weighted the windward pegs with flat stones, and cooked on the lee side where the stove could keep its flame. Pasta, a handful of dried mushrooms, and too much cheese: the meal that every camping trip eventually becomes.

Around nine the cloud lifted off the valley and the lights of three villages came out one by one, like someone walking through a house switching on lamps. We stayed out until the cold made the decision for us. Inside, the lamp on its lowest setting was enough to read by for an hour, and it was still going strong in the morning, which is more than can be said for either of us.

Breakfast was porridge and the last of the coffee. The walk down took half the time of the walk up, as it always does, and by eleven we were back at the car arguing about where to go next month. The ridge will still be there. The question is whether we can find another weekend this quiet before the snow comes and the path turns into something else entirely.
